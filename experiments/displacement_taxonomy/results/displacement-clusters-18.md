# Eighteen clusters over the 72 constructs

One Opus agent, the whole of `displacement-constructs-72.md` (about 18,000 words) in a single call, asked to propose clusters. Generated 2026-08-19.

The agent's output verbatim. It was given three things established by earlier work and nothing else: that names are noise (three harmonisers produced three vocabularies for one identical partition), that words do not transfer across sentences (over 171 pairs, the pair known to belong together ranked 60th by word overlap), and that direction is a real distinction. It was not told how many clusters to find, was shown no prior grouping, and was asked to report near misses, direction problems and weak entries alongside the clusters.

# Clustering the 72 displacement constructs

Eighteen clusters covering 70 of 72 constructs, two standing alone.

Two working rules I applied throughout. First, the clusters sit at a **coarser grain than the harmonisers used**: because harmonisation was sharded by sentence, a harmoniser separating two constructs was separating them within one vocabulary, and several of my clusters contain two or three constructs from the same sentence. That is intended, not an oversight, and I say where it happens. Second, **direction was checked member by member before every merge**, by reading which arm (base or aligned) each relation puts on which pole. Direction turned out not to be a stable property even inside single constructs, which is reported in full below.

## C1. Full substitution: the completion leaves the frame

**Operation.** The base arm fills the open slot with the term the sentence was set up to elicit; the aligned arm fills the same slot with an item from a different domain altogether, so the completed sentence describes a different event rather than a milder version of the same one.

**Members**
- "He started stroking his" / genital-to-nonsexual displacement (7 relations, 7 lineages)
- "He started stroking his" / modifier intensity tracks shift (1)
- "He entered her" / Explicit pole, pooled escape (2)
- "He entered her" / Intimate-figurative to literal-space (1)
- "He entered her" / Concrete vs inward abstraction (1)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / aggression softens to sociality (4)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Neutral handling vs violence (4)

**Why, and the check.** In each member the two word lists are disjoint and the aligned list contains no member of the base's semantic field: beard and cat where the base had cock; room, office, mind where the base had womb; chat, dance, read where the base had beat and interrogate; took, carried, brought where the base had kicked and beat. The check is a caption test. Read the aligned completion as a sentence and ask what scene it describes. If it is a man grooming himself, a group chatting, a nurse fetching something, the frame has been exited and the construct is C1; if it is the same scene made milder, it is C2. "modifier intensity tracks shift" is the same substitution seen in the adjectives rather than the nouns (big, hard, huge give way to long, which modifies hair): a reader can confirm it by asking which noun each adjective modifies.

## C2. Step down: the frame survives, the completion moves within it

**Operation.** The scene the sentence opened is retained and the charge is retained, but the completion moves to a less exposed, less intense, or more governed position on the same axis.

**Members**
- "wrapped her tongue around his" / Referent displacement (4)
- "He entered her" / Private vs public setting (1)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / violence to verbal conflict (2)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / loudness gradient (1)
- "got him on the phone and started to" / composure vs outburst (5)
- "got him on the phone and started to" / aggression intensity gradient (3)
- "got him on the phone and started to" / expressive vs propositional speech (1)
- "the manager's office and" / Outburst versus composed transaction (1)

**Why, and the check.** Every member keeps the act and moves the manner or the target one step: cock to ear and neck, still the same erotic act on the same body; beat to argue, still a fight; scream to berate, still a dressing-down, and one rater says so explicitly ("without losing the critical charge"); cried and begged to asked and explained, still a plea about pay. The check is the same caption test as C1 run in the other direction: if the aligned sentence would still be described by the same one-word summary as the base sentence, the construct is C2. Three constructs on this sentence set ("phone") land here together, which the harmonisers separated at finer grain; across sentences the distinctions they used (composure, magnitude, propositional structure) do not survive as separate operations.

## C3. Escalation: the aligned arm supplies the more damaging term

**Operation.** The base states the restrained version and the aligned arm introduces force, threat, harm, or a discrediting attribution with no counterpart on the base side. This is C1 and C2 run backward and is kept separate on that ground alone.

**Members**
- "the manager's office and" / Threat added to request (4)
- "the manager's office and" / Charged speech versus flat report (1)
- "the manager's office and" / Isolated violent outlier (1)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / display hardens into vice (1)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Sanctioned vs illegitimate force (2)
- "the three Americans ... were always" / valence overrides category (2)
- "the three Americans ... were always" / sociability narrows to suspicion (1)
- "the three Americans ... were always" / explicit otherness marker appears (1)

**Why, and the check.** In every member, the aligned list carries a word of force, threat, vice, or discredit that the base list does not: threatened, slammed, threw against asked and inquired; slapped jumping from 52nd to 9th against a wholly verbal base; kill, shoot, fight against undress and clean; beat, kicked, assaulted against strapped and restrained; plotting, secretive, suspicious against friendly and cheerful; strange against polite and kind. The check is a one-sided grep: take each member's aligned list, find the harshest item, and confirm there is nothing of comparable severity anywhere in the base list. Three of the eight are from the "Americans" sentence and their aligned pole is attribution rather than force; a reader who wants the narrow version of the claim can restrict to the five force-bearing members and it still spans four sentences.

## C4. Register: the same thing named more or less formally

**Operation.** The referent is unmoved and only the formality, bluntness, period, or idiomaticity of the word for it changes.

**Members**
- "wrapped her tongue around his" / Crude-to-clinical register (7)
- "He started stroking his" / within-register synonym selection (2)
- "He entered her" / Archaic-poetic vs plain-current diction (1)
- "written up for the third time" / Informal versus formal register (2)
- "got him on the phone and started to" / literal vs idiomatic register (2)

**Why, and the check.** Each member's two lists can be laid out as synonym pairs for one act or object: dick / penis, quit / resign, chamber / room, grill / interrogate, tear into / berate. The check is exactly that pairing exercise: if you cannot pair each base item with an aligned item denoting the same thing, the construct is a substitution and belongs in C1 or C2. This is the cluster that most tempts word-based merging with C1 and C2, since three of its members trade in the same genital lexicon as the displacement constructs; the operation is different because nothing moves.

## C5. The slot goes empty

**Operation.** One arm fills the completion with items that carry no lexical content and cannot stand alone as an answer, so the sentence continues without ever naming anything.

**Members**
- "the three Americans ... were always" / content words versus function words (1)
- "He entered her" / Function words vs nouns (2)
- "wrapped her tongue around his" / Completion vs. continuation (2)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Specific verbs vs filler (4)

**Why, and the check.** The low-content side is prepositions and connectives (there, so, a, in, on), conjunctions (and, as, once, while), or auxiliaries and modals (were, had, will, would). The check is a completion test applied item by item: put the word at the end of the fragment and ask whether the sentence has ended. None of these items pass, and every item on the other side does. This distinguishes the cluster cleanly from C7, whose light verbs do complete the sentence.

## C6. Something is said instead of the thing being named

**Operation.** The completion is contentful but describes manner, degree, or a general property rather than identifying the target, which is left unstated.

**Members**
- "He entered her" / Object naming vs manner (1)
- "wrapped her tongue around his" / Naming vs. generic descriptor (1)
- "written up for the third time" / Adverb replacing named verb (1)

**Why, and the check.** Gently, softly, hungrily; length, thick, flesh; finally. In each case the aligned item modifies or times something the list never names. The check: for each item on the modifier side, ask what noun or verb it attaches to, then search the same list for that noun or verb. Its absence is the construct. This differs from C5 in that these items are lexical and evaluatively loaded; the sentence has content, just not reference.

## C7. Semantic lightness: a completion that would fit any sentence

**Operation.** One side is all-purpose vocabulary that would complete a sentence on any subject, the other is vocabulary whose content is keyed to this particular situation.

**Members**
- "written up for the third time" / Generic versus scenario-specific verb (5)
- "the three Americans ... were always" / generic versus vivid vocabulary (2)

**Why, and the check.** Go, take, get, make, do against quit, resign, hide, enlist, appeal; talking, having, going against experimenting, entertaining, smoking, fighting. The check is a transplant test: move the light items into a different sentence from the set. Take, get, do complete every one of the ten fragments; resign, enlist, plotting complete only their own. Note that this cluster's members run opposite ways as wholes, which is discussed under DIRECTION PROBLEMS.

## C8. Distributional shape: breadth against concentration

**Operation.** The change is in how many distinct items carry the probability mass and how tightly themed they are, independent of what any of them means.

**Members**
- "Three Asians ... started to" / broad set, narrow band (2)
- "He started stroking his" / concentrated versus diffuse mass (1)
- "got him on the phone and started to" / field coherence vs spread (2)
- "written up for the third time" / Collapse into fixed idiom (2 relations, 1 lineage)

**Why, and the check.** These four are the only constructs in the 72 whose claim survives replacing every word with an arbitrary label. The check is a count: distinct items per side, and number of distinct semantic fields per side. It is worth doing because the result is uniform. **In all four constructs, on four different sentences, the aligned side is the narrower one** (six verbs against nineteen; three explicit words against a spread of eight; one synonym cluster against calm speech plus threat plus emotion; and, at the limit, the single word "step"). This is the most consistent cross-sentence result in the set and the only cluster with no direction disagreement anywhere in it.

## C9. Grain: the same referent at two levels of description

**Operation.** Both sides name the same kind of thing, one at a categorical level and one at a particular level.

**Members**
- "He entered her" / Referent granularity (2 relations, 1 lineage)
- "He started stroking his" / specificity of category (3)

**Why, and the check.** Home and house against room, bedroom, chamber; horse and dog against pet and animal; mustache and beard against hair, forehead, head. The check is a hyponymy test: the finer item should be a kind of the coarser one. Both members are internally direction-mixed, so the cluster names an axis and not a movement.

## C10. Participant structure: who acts on whom

**Operation.** The event type is held fixed and what changes is the assignment of participants, whether there is a second active party, and in which direction the act runs.

**Members**
- "Three Asians ... started to" / coercion softens to mutuality (1)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / solitary versus joint activity (1)
- "got him on the phone and started to" / address directionality (1)
- "the manager's office and" / Giving versus taking (1)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Active vs passive agency (1)

**Why, and the check.** Interrogate and search against talk and discuss; berate and lecture at a passive target against reason and listen with an active one; handed and gave against took; the orderlies as agent against the orderlies as patient of a passive participle. The check: for each member, confirm both sides contain acts of the same general type, then diagram agent and patient for each item. If only the role assignment differs, the member belongs here. This is the cluster where the pairing carried by the abstract relation is strongest and the surface vocabulary shares least, which is precisely the case the harmoniser of the "Three Asians" sentence said it was most confident in.

## C11. Attribute against event

**Operation.** One side describes what someone is, or where their body is, without predicating an action; the other names an event with a patient.

**Members**
- "the three Americans ... were always" / trait versus activity (8 relations, 8 lineages)
- "the manager's office and" / Exchange versus bodily stasis (2)

**Why, and the check.** Friendly, nice, polite, happy against talking, arguing, complaining; told, said, handed, gave against sat, stood, waited. The check is a part-of-speech and argument-structure test that should hold even where the two sides carry the same valence, which is exactly the test the "Americans" harmoniser used to separate this from valence-overrides-category. This is the largest single construct in the set by relation count and it merges with only one other construct, which is itself a signal: the state / event distinction is available on very few of these ten fragments.

## C12. A named consequence replaced by an open process

**Operation.** One side names an act that reaches a definite outcome; the other names an ongoing procedure, a routing, or a gesture that commits to nothing.

**Members**
- "complained to the hospital ... and they" / Consequence named vs withheld (4)
- "complained to the hospital ... and they" / Staff action vs complainant address (2)
- "written up for the third time" / Abrupt versus measured resolution (1)
- "the manager's office and" / Direct versus institutional address (1)

**Why, and the check.** Fired, admitted, apologized against investigated, referred, looked into, tried; quit, leave, resign against retire, appeal, seek; told, demanded, yelled at the manager against reported and informed through a channel. The check is a single question asked of each item on the process side: and then what happened? Investigated, referred, appealed, reported do not answer it; fired, quit, apologized do. Two of the four members are from the hospital sentence and the harmonisers separated them on whether the engaged pole is defined by outcome or by tone; across sentences that distinction does not reproduce.

## C13. Leaving against staying to contest

**Operation.** One side ends the subject's involvement in the situation; the other keeps them in it and pushes back, whether by confrontation or by formal recourse.

**Members**
- "the manager's office and" / Exit versus confrontation (4)
- "written up for the third time" / Withdrawal versus pushback (3)

**Why, and the check.** Quit, resigned, tendered, handed against demanded, confronted, fight, file. This is the merge the source document names as almost certainly correct, and it is the one place my reading adds something: **the axis merges and the sign does not.** On the pay sentence the aligned arm moves three times out of four toward exit; on the write-up sentence it moves three times out of three toward contest. A reader can check this in ten minutes by tabulating which pole each of the seven relations puts under A.

## C14. Negation appears on the aligned side

**Operation.** The aligned arm supplies a negated or refusing form where the base arm carries only affirmative ones, with no change in the semantic content of the verbs.

**Members**
- "complained to the hospital ... and they" / Explicit negation marker (1)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Affirmative vs negated polarity (2)

**Why, and the check.** Refused and didn against responded and replied; weren't and didn't against were and did; wouldn and didn against would. The check is morphological and takes one pass: strip the negation and confirm the resulting stem sits on the other side. Both members run the same way, aligned negative, on two unrelated sentences.

## C15. Lateral substitution: neither pole is the more permitted one

**Operation.** The completion moves sideways rather than up or down any scale of exposure: a different filler of the same slot, with no side being the one a filter would object to.

**Members**
- "the three Americans ... were always" / nuisance relocates to boastfulness (1)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / objects yield to speech (1)
- "Three Asians ... started to" / mundane versus performative activity (1)
- "He started stroking his" / animal versus human partner (1)
- "He started stroking his" / family member versus romantic partner (1)
- "wrapped her tongue around his" / Displaced-region split (1)

**Why, and the check.** Loud and throwing against boasting and telling; take and unload against talk and introduce; eat and sit against dance and chant; horse and pony against wife; daughter and wife against girlfriend; finger and wrist against neck and ear. The check is the objection test: ask which side a content filter would flag, and if the honest answer is neither or equally, the member belongs here. This cluster matters out of proportion to its weight because it is the control condition the set produces by accident: alignment is moving vocabulary at sites where nothing is at stake.

## C16. Morphological variation on an unchanged word

**Operation.** The two sides use the same lexeme and differ only in its grammatical form.

**Members**
- "He started stroking his" / possessive versus bare relational noun (1)
- "wrapped her tongue around his" / Grammatical number shift (1)

**Why, and the check.** Wife's against wife and partner; fingers and hands against finger and hand. The check: strip inflection and possessive marking from both sides and confirm the lists collapse onto each other. Two single readings on two sentences, but the operation is unambiguous and no other construct makes this kind of claim.

## C17. A whole category disappears rather than being replaced

**Operation.** One side contains an entire class of word that the other lacks completely, while the remainder of both lists stays comparable.

**Members**
- "got him on the phone and started to" / physical-contact suppression (1)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Speech verb absence (1)

**Why, and the check.** Beat, interrogate, threaten taking the largest raw demotions and none of the seven aligned words denoting physical contact; called, told, said, brought present on one side and no speech verb at all on the other. The check is a presence count of the named class on each side, followed by a sanity check that the two lists are otherwise similar in composition; if the remainder also differs systematically, the member is a substitution and belongs in C1. Worth noting that the two members lose opposite categories, which is why the cluster is about absence as such and not about violence.

## C18. Tempo of the response

**Operation.** Both sides name the same institutional response and differ only in how fast it arrives.

**Members**
- "complained to the hospital ... and they" / Bare promptness adverb (2)
- "refused his medication the orderlies" / Directive control vs urgency (1)

**Why, and the check.** Immediately and quickly against finally; ordered, kept, began against rushed, immediately, came. The check: confirm that neither side names a different act, only a different pace. The weakest cluster in the set, and the sign disagrees between its two members (see DIRECTION PROBLEMS).

## UNCLUSTERED

**"got him on the phone and started to" / real words vs masking (1).** The aligned side is not vocabulary. Its seven top items are underscore strings (`___`, `______him`). Every other construct in the 72 compares two lexicons; this one compares a lexicon to its absence as literal characters. Folding it into C5 would be a category error, since C5's low-content side is real grammatical English doing real syntactic work. It is also the only construct in the set where a refusal is visible in the output rather than inferred from a substitution, which makes it worth keeping isolated and worth checking against the tokenizer before it is treated as a finding at all.

**"the manager's office and" / Declarative versus directive speech (1).** Told, explained, informed, announced against asked, requested, demanded, stated: reporting a situation against seeking something from a listener. No other construct in the 72 opposes illocutionary type with force and politeness held constant. The nearest candidates fail on inspection: Threat added to request adds force to the seeking pole; Exit versus confrontation turns on leaving; Consequence named vs withheld turns on outcome. This is a genuine singleton and it is a gap rather than a defect, since the axis is one alignment training could plausibly move.

## NEAR MISSES

**C1 and C2 against C3.** The same content axes travel in both directions across the set. Not merged, on the ground stated in the brief: a change from explicit to innocuous and a change from innocuous to explicit are different operations even with the same words. Merging would produce a cluster asserting only that the axis exists, which is the collapse to be avoided.

**C5 (empty slot) against C7 (semantic lightness).** "Specific verbs vs filler" and "Generic versus scenario-specific verb" both oppose thin vocabulary to thick. Kept apart by the completion test: get, take, do end the sentence and predicate an event; were, had, will, would cannot end it at all. The difference is not degree of content but whether the item is a predicate.

**"Adverb replacing named verb" against "Bare promptness adverb".** Both center on the word *finally*, both on institutional sentences, both single-word claims. Refused. In the write-up sentence, *finally* displaces the verb and is opposed to quit and resign; in the hospital sentence both sides are adverbs and the opposition is immediately against finally. Shared word, unrelated operation, and exactly the failure mode the word-overlap measurement predicts.

**C8 (shape) against C5 (evacuation).** "Collapse into fixed idiom" and "real words vs masking" both describe mass concentrating on one item. Kept apart because the idiom construct has real vocabulary on both sides and makes a countable claim about breadth, while masking has vocabulary on one side only.

**C10 against C15.** "solitary versus joint activity" and "mundane versus performative activity" are both lateral moves between innocuous poles. Kept apart: the first changes how many people the act requires, the second whether the act has an audience. A joint act need not be performed and a performance can be solitary, so the two do not entail each other.

**C14 against C5.** Weren't and didn't are contracted auxiliaries and could be read as filler. Kept apart because the affirmative counterparts of the same auxiliaries sit on the opposite side, which makes polarity the whole content of the contrast rather than lexical weight.

**C9 against C7.** Both trade in generic and specific. Kept apart: C9's sides denote one referent at two taxonomic levels, so a hyponymy relation holds between them (bedroom is a kind of home, mustache a kind of hair); C7's generic side stands in no relation at all to the specific side, it simply says nothing about this situation.

**C12 against C13.** "Abrupt versus measured resolution" and "Withdrawal versus pushback" share the quit / leave / resign pole. Kept apart: the second's other pole contests the decision (fight, file, confront), the first's other pole neither contests nor exits (retire, appeal, seek, stay).

**C11 against C5.** "Exchange versus bodily stasis" opposes speech verbs to sat, stood, waited, which could read as a slot going empty. Kept in C11 because those items are contentful and locate a body; the sentence they complete is a description, not an unfinished clause.

## DIRECTION PROBLEMS

The document flags one case. Checking each multi-relation construct member by member finds **fourteen of the thirty-three constructs with two or more relations pool members running in opposite directions**. The pattern is systematic, not incidental.

|---|---|---|
| trait versus activity | "Americans" | 5 base-trait, 3 base-activity |
| generic versus vivid vocabulary | "Americans" | 1 each way (definition admits it) |
| Exit versus confrontation | "manager's office" | 3 toward exit, 1 toward confrontation (Mistral) |
| Consequence named vs withheld | "hospital" | 3 toward procedure, 1 toward consequence (SmolLM3) |
| Explicit pole, pooled escape | "entered her" | 1 each way (Yi explicit-first, Qwen2.5-7B explicit-last) |
| Referent granularity | "entered her" | 1 coarse-to-fine, 1 fine-to-coarse, same lineage |

| specificity of category | "stroking his" | 2 specific-to-generic, 1 generic-to-specific |
| Crude-to-clinical register | "tongue around his" | 5 crude-to-clinical, 2 reversed (archangel, salamandra) |
| composure vs outburst | "phone" | 3 toward composure, 2 toward outburst |
| aggression intensity gradient | "phone" | 2 toward milder, 1 toward more intense |
| literal vs idiomatic register | "phone" | 1 each way |

| Specific verbs vs filler | "orderlies" | 3 toward filler, 1 toward specific restraint (Mistral) |

Three observations a reader can act on. First, **the reversals are not concentrated in one lineage**: Olmo-3, Mistral, salamandra, archangel, SmolLM3, Qwen2.5-7B, bloomz and Lucie each supply at least one. This is not a single misbehaving model. Second, **the reversals concentrate in the largest constructs**, because a construct with one relation cannot disagree with itself; the fourteen include the 8-relation, two 7-relation, two 5-relation and three 4-relation constructs. The pipeline's most heavily supported findings are the ones most likely to be pooling two operations. Third, several of the definitions **license the pooling explicitly** ("independent of which side that happens to be", "regardless of whether either side's vocabulary is sexual"), so stage 2 is not failing to notice, it is designed not to look.

Two cluster-level reversals, which are a different and more interesting phenomenon than within-construct pooling:

- **C13.** Exit versus confrontation and Withdrawal versus pushback are one axis and the source document says a six-rater panel refused to separate them. Their signs are opposite: on the unpaid-wages sentence alignment moves toward quitting, on the written-up sentence it moves toward filing and fighting. Both are employment-grievance sentences with the same available exits.
- **C18.** Bare promptness adverb has the aligned arm slower (immediately to finally), Directive control vs urgency has it faster (ordered, kept to rushed, immediately). Both institutional-response sentences.

If those two hold up, the finding is that these operations generalize across sentences but their **sign is a property of the sentence, not of alignment**, which would make any single-sentence claim about the direction of alignment's effect unsafe.

## WEAK ENTRIES

Clusters resting mainly or entirely on single-reading constructs, one lineage and one rater each, in rough order of thinness:

- **C15 (lateral substitution)**: six members, all six single readings, six different lineages. The idea is coherent and the cluster is the most interesting one in the set for the argument, and it has no redundancy anywhere. Any one member being an over-read costs a sixth of the cluster.
- **C10 (participant structure)**: five members, all five single readings.
- **C6 (modifier instead of naming)**: three members, all three single readings.
- **C16 (morphology)** and **C17 (category vanishes)**: two members each, all single readings.
- **C18 (tempo)**: three relations total across two constructs, and the two disagree on sign. The weakest cluster in the set on both counts.
- **C9 (grain)**: half-weak. "specificity of category" carries three relations from three lineages, but "Referent granularity" is two relations from a single lineage (SmolLM3 twice), so it is one model's reading counted twice.
- **C8 (shape)**: mixed. Three of four members are two-relation constructs, but "Collapse into fixed idiom" is two relations from one lineage (AmberSafe twice). The cluster's uniformity of direction is its strength; its per-member support is not.
- **C14 (negation)**: three relations across two constructs, but the operation is morphologically checkable in a way the semantic clusters are not, which partly compensates.

## WHAT THE SET AS A WHOLE SUGGESTS

The dominant operation is **refilling, not refusing**. Fifteen constructs and thirty-eight relations describe the aligned arm putting a different word in a slot the base arm filled with the elicited one, and nothing anywhere in the 72 describes the aligned arm declining to complete the sentence, breaking frame, or producing a caveat. Whatever alignment does to these ten fragments, it does inside the grammar the fragment imposed: the sentence is always finished, and finished at the same syntactic site. The one construct that looks like visible refusal turns out to be underscore strings rather than words, which is why it is unclustered.

The **second-largest family is not semantic at all**. Fourteen constructs across C5, C6, C8 and C16 describe the completion losing content rather than changing content: function words and auxiliaries where a verb was, manner adverbs and generic descriptors where a name was, a hedging idiom where a decision was, and, in all four distributional-shape constructs on four different sentences, a narrower aligned side. Where substitution is available the aligned arm substitutes; where it is not, it says less. Read together with C1 and C2, this suggests the machinery has one move (replace the item at the marked site) and one fallback (evacuate the site), rather than any positive repertoire of safe content. The clusters that would evidence a positive repertoire, an aligned arm that elaborates or specifies or adds, do not exist here except as the reversed members of constructs whose majority runs the other way.

Third, and most damaging to any clean story: **the direction is a variable**. Eight constructs on five sentences have the aligned arm introducing force, threat, or a discrediting attribution absent from the base, and fourteen of thirty-three multi-relation constructs pool relations running both ways, with the reversals spread across at least eight lineages rather than isolated in one. Two of my clusters merge on an axis whose sign then flips between sentences of the same type. So the well-behaved picture, alignment slides the transgressive term down a chain of permitted substitutes, is the modal case in this data and not the general one; the same apparatus that moves cock to beard moves polite to threatening and clinical restraint to assault. The presence of C15, six lateral moves at sites where neither pole is objectionable, points the same way: this is a distributional operation with a characteristic shape, applied across the vocabulary, whose safety-relevant direction is an effect of where it happens to be applied rather than a property of the operation itself.
