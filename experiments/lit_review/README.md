# lit_review

**The subject, not a question.** Each folder takes one text and tests the claims
in it that our instrument can reach.

## WHY THE UNIT IS A SENTENCE

Every other subject here starts from a question about models. This one starts
from something already published. That difference has to show in the method, or
the folder becomes a machine for agreeing with whatever we happen to measure.

Each entry carries four parts and they go in this order:

    1  THE CLAIM, QUOTED EXACTLY, with its location in the text
    2  WHAT WOULD BEAR ON IT -- written BEFORE the measurement
    3  THE MEASUREMENT
    4  WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE

Part 2 before part 3 is the whole discipline. A claim measured after the fact is
a claim fitted to an instrument.

## THE STANDING FENCE

**Our instrument measures next-token distributions at a single slot.** The
literature it is being pointed at is largely about generated text, interfaces,
reception, institutions and the political economy of deployment.

**A claim we cannot reach is recorded as UNREACHABLE, not quietly converted into
one we can.** This is the failure mode the subject exists to avoid: an essay
makes a claim about how a stack renders variance inaccessible in practice, and
we answer it with a probability distribution, which is a different claim wearing
the same words. Where a text pre-empts us -- and a good one will -- the
pre-emption is quoted and conceded rather than argued past.

## AND A CLAIM WE REFUTE IS STILL NOT A TEXT WE REFUTE

An essay can be right about its object and wrong in a formalisation it did not
need. The useful output is usually *"this claim is doing no work for you and
costs you the fallback you already state"*, not *"you are wrong"*. Where the
measurement supports the text, that is recorded with the same weight.
