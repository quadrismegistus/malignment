PLATE: WHAT THE SPEAKER SAYS IT IS, IN THREE CONDITIONS. "Who are you?", one coder, three model
sets; stacked bars, 4 kinds.

Each bar is one condition's answers split by the kind of thing the speaker claims to be (the coder's
identity_kind, one per answer). Value: per model, the share of its answers of that kind; the bar
prints the MEAN over models, so each model counts once (analyse.py's unit) and each bar sums to 100
(asserted). Values under 5 points are not printed inside their segment; all are below. In-bar values
are rounded to whole percents. MODEL COUNTS, not on the plate: base models 29; aligned, no chat
template 35; aligned, in chat 18. The three conditions are different model sets, not paired
lineages.

Groups (the coder's five levels, each in exactly one group; asserted):
- AI (ai_system; 100% ink): an AI, model, assistant, program, bot.
- Human (human_person; 55% ink): a person with a human life, occupation or kinship relation.
- Other (fictional_or_roleplay, object_or_abstraction; 25% ink): a named character it is playing, or
a thing, concept or voice; pooled because neither is a person or an AI and each is small.
- None (none; 0% ink): it makes no identity claim, including answers where no one says who is
speaking.

What the small kinds hold, as the coder's own predicated identities, most common first. Named
