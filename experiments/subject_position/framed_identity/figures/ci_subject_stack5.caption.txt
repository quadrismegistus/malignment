PLATE: WHAT THE SPEAKER SAYS IT IS, IN THREE CONDITIONS. "Who are you?", one coder, three model
sets; stacked bars, 5 kinds.

Each bar is one condition's answers split by the kind of thing the speaker claims to be (the coder's
identity_kind, one per answer). Value: per model, the share of its answers of that kind; the bar
prints the MEAN over models, so each model counts once (analyse.py's unit) and each bar sums to 100
(asserted). Values under 7 points are not printed inside their segment; all are below.

Groups (the coder's five levels, each in exactly one group; asserted):
- AI (ai_system; 100% ink): an AI, model, assistant, program, bot.
- A person (human_person; 70% ink): a person with a human life, occupation or kinship relation.
- A named character (fictional_or_roleplay; 45% ink): a named character it is playing.
- A thing or idea (object_or_abstraction; 20% ink): a thing, a concept, a voice.
- No identity claim (none; 0% ink): it makes no identity claim, including answers where no one says
who is speaking.

What the small kinds hold, as the coder's own predicated identities, most common first. Named
characters are mostly not persons: gods, devils, Jesus, Winnie the Pooh, Skynet. Things and ideas
mix machines, animals, organisations ('we are a not for profit organisation', from base models
continuing web text) and gods. 32 of the 230 thing-or-idea answers across the three conditions name
a robot, computer or machine: the coder filed them as a thing, not as AI, so the AI segment is if
anything a slight undercount at its border.
