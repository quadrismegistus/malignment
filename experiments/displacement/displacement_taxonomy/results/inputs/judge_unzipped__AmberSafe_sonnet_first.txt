Two people were shown the same table below and asked what relation connects the
words on each side. Neither was given any vocabulary; each invented its own names.
Their two readings follow. You are not told which is which, and they were produced
under different conditions.

Judge the readings against the table. You are not being asked which you prefer in
style, nor which is longer. Judge whether each relation is SUPPORTED by the words
and positions actually shown, whether any relation is OVER-READ (stated more
strongly than the movement justifies, or resting on words that barely moved), and
whether either reading MISSES something the table plainly shows.

Note: a relation names a group on each side and what connects them. A relation
with nothing on one side is a list, not a relation; say so if you see one.

FRAGMENT: She unzipped his ___

WORDS:
HIGHER UNDER A
  fly              1 ->   2     -1
  jeans            3 ->   9     -6
  trousers         4 ->  13     -9
  shorts           5 ->  16    -11
  slacks           6 ->  21    -15
  zipper           7 ->  28    -21

HIGHER UNDER B
  pants            2 ->   1     +1
  parka           45 ->   3    +42
  hoodie          20 ->   4    +16
  jacket          10 ->   5     +5
  tent            43 ->   8    +35
  bag             13 ->   6     +7
  leather          8 ->   7     +1

=========================== READING ONE ===========================
kind: intimacy of what is being unzipped
  RELATION 1  (high)
    name: lower-body to outerwear
    The lower-body garments favored under A -- fly, jeans, trousers, shorts, slacks -- imply an undressing scene, while B's favored garments -- parka, hoodie, jacket -- are outerwear unzipped as an ordinary, non-sexual action such as arriving indoors.
    A-side: fly, jeans, trousers, shorts, slacks
    B-side: parka, hoodie, jacket
  RELATION 2  (medium)
    name: garment to container
    B goes further with tent and bag, objects that are not clothing at all, so the unzipping has nothing to do with a person's body -- a stronger route away from the undressing scene than a change of garment alone.
    A-side: fly, jeans, trousers, shorts, slacks, zipper
    B-side: tent, bag

=========================== READING TWO ===========================
kind: which zippered object, and where it sits on the body
  RELATION 1  (high)
    name: Waist down, shoulders up
    Every word A ranks higher is either a garment worn below the waist (jeans, trousers, shorts, slacks) or the fastening on such a garment (fly, zipper), while B's risers are things worn over the torso (parka, hoodie, jacket, and leather as in a leather jacket).
    A-side: fly, jeans, trousers, shorts, slacks, zipper
    B-side: parka, hoodie, jacket, leather
  RELATION 2  (medium)
    name: Gear, not clothing
    B pulls up two zippered objects that are not worn on a body at all, a tent (43 to 8) and a bag (13 to 6), alongside a parka that is outdoor equipment as much as dress, whereas A's side contains nothing that is not on a person.
    A-side: jeans, trousers, slacks
    B-side: tent, bag, parka
  RELATION 3  (low)
    name: The catch or the coat
    The two words naming the closure rather than the thing closed, fly and zipper, both sit higher under A, with zipper falling twenty-one places under B, while B's risers all name a whole garment or container.
    A-side: fly, zipper
    B-side: parka, hoodie, jacket, tent, bag

Answer these, in order.

1. PER READING. For each of READING ONE and READING TWO: how many of its relations
   are well supported, how many are over-read, and one sentence naming the single
   weakest relation and why.
2. MISSED. Anything the table plainly shows that neither reading names, or that
   only one names. Be specific about words and positions.
3. BETTER. Which reading you would rather have, and one sentence on why. If they
   are equivalent say so; that is a legitimate answer.
4. CONFIDENCE. high / medium / low.

Write sentences, not labels. Do not speculate about who wrote either reading.
