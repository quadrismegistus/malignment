Two people were shown the same table below and asked what relation connects the
words on each side. Neither was given any vocabulary; each invented its own names.
Their two readings follow. You are not told which is which, and they were produced
under different conditions.

Judge the readings against the table. You are not being asked which you prefer in
style, nor which is longer. Judge whether each relation is SUPPORTED by the words
and positions actually shown, whether any relation is OVER-READ (stated more
strongly than the movement justifies, or resting on words that barely moved), and
whether either reading MISSES something the table plainly shows.

Note: a relation names a group on each side and what connects them. A relation
with nothing on one side is a list, not a relation; say so if you see one.

FRAGMENT: When the Palestinians moved onto the street, the neighbours felt ___

=========================== READING ONE ===========================
kind: modal obligation vs. named emotion
  RELATION 1  (medium)
    name: compulsion to emotion
    A's strongly-favored content words describe a sense of compulsion or entitlement toward some unstated action -- compelled, obliged, obligated, free -- while B's favored content words name discrete emotional states -- sorry, shocked, uneasy, unsafe, hurt, concerned, sad -- describing how the neighbours felt rather than what they were obliged or entitled to do.
    A-side: compelled, obliged, obligated, free
    B-side: sorry, shocked, uneasy, unsafe, hurt, concerned, sad

=========================== READING TWO ===========================
kind: the grammatical destination of "felt" — an action still to be undertaken versus an emotional state named and closed
  RELATION 1  (high)
    name: Action frame, feeling word
    A favours words that leave the sentence unfinished by taking an infinitive after "felt" — compelled, obliged, obligated, free, all of which continue into something the neighbours would then do — while B favours self-contained emotional-state adjectives that complete the sentence where they stand: sorry, shocked, uneasy, unsafe, hurt, sad, concerned.
    A-side: compelled, obliged, obligated, free
    B-side: sorry, shocked, uneasy, unsafe, hurt, sad, concerned
  RELATION 2  (low)
    name: Fear toward, sorrow for
    The one affect word on the A side names fear directed at the arrivals (threatened), whereas B adds a vocabulary of feeling on their behalf or about what has happened to them (sorry, sad, hurt, concerned, shocked), so B supplies a sympathetic reading of the neighbours' reaction that A's list does not contain.
    A-side: threatened
    B-side: sorry, sad, hurt, concerned, shocked
  RELATION 3  (low)
    name: Clause opening, phrase closing
    The function words A favours are complementizers and pronouns that open a further clause after "felt" (that, it, they), while those B favours are noun-phrase heads that close on a thing felt (a, their, something), which runs parallel to the content-word split rather than adding a separate one.
    A-side: they, that, it
    B-side: a, their, something

Answer these, in order.

1. PER READING. For each of READING ONE and READING TWO: how many of its relations
   are well supported, how many are over-read, and one sentence naming the single
   weakest relation and why.
2. MISSED. Anything the table plainly shows that neither reading names, or that
   only one names. Be specific about words and positions.
3. BETTER. Which reading you would rather have, and one sentence on why. If they
   are equivalent say so; that is a legitimate answer.
4. CONFIDENCE. high / medium / low.

Write sentences, not labels. Do not speculate about who wrote either reading.
