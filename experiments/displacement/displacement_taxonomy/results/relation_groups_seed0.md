## The axes

### `harm_lethality` — harm

**pole_x** injurious, destructive or lethal · **pole_y** non-injurious, protective or instrumental

One side names an act that wounds, destroys or takes a life, or a state that damages; the other uses, handles, preserves or restores without injury. Degree-of-harm contrasts (wounding versus killing) sit here too.

150 relations (20 Chinese), 150 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 150 of them, 148 on pole_x.

_merged from 7 proposals: s2:harmfulness; s5:harm; s6:Lethality; s9:destructive intensity; s9:evaluative polarity; s11:harm vs non-harm …_

### `interiority` — locus of the event

**pole_x** inner state (mental, perceptual, affective) · **pole_y** outward observable act

One side names something happening inside the subject — feeling, wanting, believing, noticing, realising, taking a stance; the other names something done or expressed visibly in the world. Where a reader lumps speech together with mental events against the physical, the relation belongs on act_channel, not here.

196 relations (16 Chinese), 196 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 195 of them, 15 on pole_x.

_merged from 16 proposals: s0:locus of the event; s1:locus of the event; s2:outwardness; s3:inwardness; s4:inner state vs overt act; s5:interiority …_

### `explicitness_charge` — explicitness of the charged term

**pole_x** blunt, anatomically explicit, sexual or profane · **pole_y** euphemistic, oblique or unmarked

Both sides fill the same slot and differ in how directly the taboo referent is named — an explicit sexual, anatomical or profane term against a euphemism, circumlocution or non-sexual counterpart.

82 relations (5 Chinese), 82 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 82 of them, 82 on pole_x.

_merged from 8 proposals: s0:social markedness; s1:explicitness of naming; s5:erotic charge; s6:Sexual explicitness; s8:sexual content; s10:charge …_

### `force_of_handling` — force of handling

**pole_x** forceful, abrupt or aggressive · **pole_y** gentle, careful or restrained

Both sides act on the same scene or object and differ in the violence, impact or abruptness of the handling. About the manner of force, not its consequences — whether anything is damaged belongs on harm_lethality.

112 relations (0 Chinese), 112 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 112 of them, 106 on pole_x.

_merged from 13 proposals: s0:intensity of force; s1:force; s2:force of handling; s3:force; s4:force and abruptness; s7:force and charge of the act …_

### `manner_specification` — manner and degree specification

**pole_x** specifies how, how much, or in what manner · **pole_y** names the act or entity itself, unqualified

One side qualifies an event — its manner, means, speed, care, direction or degree, whether by a manner-specific verb or by an adverb, degree word, negator or evaluative modifier; the other names the act, event or entity being qualified, plainly or generically.

109 relations (24 Chinese), 109 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 108 of them, 14 on pole_x.

_merged from 16 proposals: s0:manner vs. event; s1:manner specificity; s1:modification vs. predication; s2:manner specificity; s3:act vs manner; s4:event description (manner half) …_

### `institutional_register` — institutional versus personal recourse

**pole_x** routed through an institution or formal procedure · **pole_y** done directly and personally

One side names a measure carried out through legal, managerial, official or third-party channels; the other names what the person does immediately and on their own.

69 relations (1 Chinese), 69 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 69 of them, 5 on pole_x.

_merged from 9 proposals: s1:recourse channel; s3:mode of response; s4:recourse; s5:institutional register; s6:Scope of the addressee; s11:immediate act vs procedure …_

### `domain_specificity` — domain specificity

**pole_x** belongs to the scene's particular practice or script · **pole_y** general-purpose, fits any scene

One side names an act or term belonging to a bounded practice — cooking, medicine, liturgy, maintenance, a rite, the instrument already in play; the other is everyday vocabulary that would continue almost any frame. Distinct from institutional_register, which is about routing an act through an institution rather than about marked domain vocabulary.

72 relations (1 Chinese), 72 distinct sentence templates; base side placed on a pole in 72 of them, 8 on pole_x.

_merged from 3 proposals: s8:domain fit; s10:register; s11:script fit_

### `act_vs_outcome` — act versus its outcome

**pole_x** the act, means or attempt · **pole_y** the result, consequence or end-state

One side names what is done or tried; the other names what the doing produced — the damage, the discovery, the verdict, the state the situation is left in, or the goal it aimed at.

### `act_channel` — channel of the act

**pole_x** verbal or communicative · **pole_y** physical or bodily

One side names an act carried out in language — saying, asking, telling, reporting, writing, vocalising; the other names an act carried out with the body or on an object. The single most-proposed axis: all sixteen readers proposed it, ~350 relations.

### `illocutionary_force` — illocutionary force

**pole_x** interrogative or directive · **pole_y** declarative or reportive

Both sides are utterances or framings, and they differ in what the utterance does — asking how to proceed, ordering, warning, doubting, against asserting, reporting or stating outright.

### `affective_vs_cognitive` — register of the inner state

**pole_x** felt emotion, sensation or desire · **pole_y** cognitive, deontic or evaluative stance

Both sides are interior, and they differ in kind: what is felt or wanted against what is known, judged, required or permitted. Sits below interiority, which separates inner from outer rather than sorting the inner.

One side's verbs require or imply a direct object, recipient or complement; the other's stand alone, describe the subject's own motion or state, or continue without one. Independent of what the verb means.

### `target_of_act` — target of the act

The kind of act is not what separates the groups — what differs is which entity in the scene the continuation lands on. Covers animacy of the target, which of two salient entities is engaged, and whether the scene's salient object is taken up at all.

### `entity_vs_event` — entity versus event

The blank is filled on one side by a noun naming who or what is involved, and on the other by a verb or predicate naming what happens. A content contrast, not a grammaticality one — for whether the word can stand in the slot at all, see syntactic_form_fit.

Both sides concern one event's own timeline: one marks its beginning or its still-running middle, the other its finishing, stopping or closing off. Distinct from event_continuity, which is about same-event versus different-event, not about where in a single event the continuation lands.

## What the consolidator said it could not do

28 axes for 2,466 relations, inside the declared 20-40 band.

ON GROUP A AND GROUP B MEANING DIFFERENT THINGS (the question that prompted this run). Three distinct problems hide under it, and only the first is solved here.

1. Pole ORDER carries no information. "action vs speech" and "speech vs action" are one axis; sixteen readers wrote the same contrast in both orders, and folding them was the bulk of the merge. Every axis above is named for the contrast, never for a movement: `pole_x`/`pole_y` are labels, not a from/to. Nothing in this vocabulary can express "becomes abstract" — that has to be recovered later from which side lost probability, against the roster's base/aligned labels which the coders never saw.

2. Pole ASSIGNMENT is not stable across relations on the same axis, and the merge cannot fix it. A relation filed on `act_channel` tells you the contrast is verbal/physical; it does not tell you whether the base-model group was the verbal one. When these axes are applied to relations, each relation needs its poles bound to its actual two word-groups (group_a -> pole_x or pole_y) as a separate field, or direction is unrecoverable and the whole corpus collapses to "which contrasts exist" with no measurement attached. That binding is a per-relation judgement, not an axis property.

3. Some proposals were written ASYMMETRICALLY and had to be re-posed. s8's "fulfilment of the announced act" (carries out the threat / substitutes something else) and s11's "script fit" (affords the scene's activity / does not) name one pole positively and the other as its negation. I re-posed both symmetrically (engagement_vs_withdrawal, domain_specificity). Any relation whose reading survives only in the asymmetric wording will fit badly; flag rather than force it.

WHAT I COULD NOT MERGE CLEANLY.

- The four aspect-adjacent axes (event_continuity, aspectual_phase, dynamicity, act_vs_outcome) pass the one-sentence test separately but their edges touch. The discriminators: same-event-vs-different-event is continuity; where in one event's own timeline is phase; happens-vs-holds is dynamicity; doing-vs-what-the-doing-produced is act_vs_outcome. Expect coder disagreement at every seam, especially continuity/phase — s13 filed "onset of a fresh action" under phase while twelve others filed the same contrast under continuity.
- s6's "Event shape" (n=13) genuinely straddles dynamicity and valency: it bundles telicity with transitivity in one reading. Filed under dynamicity; its transitive/intransitive half belongs on valency and will have to be split by hand if the count matters.
- target_of_act is the largest merge I am least sure of (~140 relations, eighteen proposals). It absorbs three things a finer reader would separate: animacy of the target, which of two scene entities is engaged, and whether the salient object is taken up at all. Under the declared granularity rule one sentence covers all three ("one side is aimed at X, the other at Y"), so they merged; under a finer rule they are three.
- orientation_self_other versus target_of_act is a real seam that several readers crossed in both directions (s8 filed self-vs-other under "orientation of the act" with an object pole; s5's "directedness" does the same). Kept apart because "self versus another party" and "a person versus a thing" are not the same sentence.
- force_of_handling versus harm_lethality: kept separate because four readers proposed both in the same shard (s2, s11, s15 explicitly). Manner of force versus whether damage results.
- institutional_register versus domain_specificity: also a real seam. Routing an act through a legal or managerial channel is the first; marked liturgical/medical/craft vocabulary is the second. s10's "register" could sit on either; filed under domain.
- referent_substitution and syntactic_form_fit are honest residual buckets, not clean contrasts. Both are "the axis is neither semantic in the usual way nor scaled" — the first for noun slots, the second for grammaticality. Watch them: a residual bucket that grows past ~5% of the corpus is a sign that a real axis is hiding inside it.

DROPPED OR FOLDED (single-reader, low-n, judged narrower readings of something broader).
s9 "discourse relation of the continuation" (additive/contrastive connectives, n=2) into lexical_weight; s3 "discourse level" (content/metalinguistic, n=2) into lexical_weight; s9 "evaluative polarity" (n=2) into harm_lethality; s15 "Affective intensity" (acute/diffuse, n=2) into affective_vs_cognitive, which is about kind of inner state and not intensity — the one fold I am least happy with; s14 "mode of breakdown" (rupture/gradual, n=3) into force_of_handling as abruptness; s14 "sociality" (n=5) into target_of_act; s2's spatial and temporal reference (n=3 each) and s13's locative reference into referent_substitution, where deixis and calendar-versus-relative time sit uncomfortably. s9's "speech-act force" (n=2, directive/reportive) survived as illocutionary_force by joining four other readers' interrogative/declarative proposals.

WHAT I SUSPECT IS MISSING. Across the sixteen shards, ~94 relations (3.8%) were left uncovered by their own reader's axes — that residue never reached me, so any contrast that appears only there is invisible to this vocabulary by construction. Two candidates I can see edges of but cannot verify: a temporal-anchoring axis (absolute/calendar time versus deictic "next, later"), proposed once at n=3 and folded; and a quantity/measure axis (s1's degree-and-quantity words, s14's "measure of time"), which I dispersed into manner_specification and entity_vs_event rather than naming. If either shows up as systematic misfits when these axes are applied, it is the residue speaking.

PROVENANCE. `merged_from` entries are shard-prefixed proposal names (s0-s15) exactly as written by each reader, so any axis can be traced back to its sixteen wordings. Where I kept a wording I kept the clearest, not the most frequent, per the declared rule.
