words of a concreteness norm (z-scored median of human concreteness and imageability norms, extended
to period vocabulary); positive is concrete, negative abstract. Bottom: per text, the share of
content words that are words of clear positive or negative valence (a cleaned Warriner et al. 2013
lexicon plus rater-confirmed period vocabulary; above 6 or below 4 on the 1-9 scale). Gray points:
decade medians over 9,836 Chadwyck and Chicago novels; black line: lowess (span 0.3). Lines: model
fiction, national stories from a neutral prompt (judged proper stories), each model-condition as one
text, median over the lineages run in that condition (34 base, 36 aligned, 27 prefilled, 24 chat).
Evaluative language's historical fall and its rise under alignment both survive controlling for
concreteness (abstraction's text score, partialled out by the slope within decade or within
condition): the fall of evaluative language from the 1760s to the 1950s keeps 65% of its size, and
aligned exceeds base in 25 of 32 paired lineages (raw), 20 of 23 (prefilled) and 17 of 20 (chat),
keeping 78%, 80% and 75% of the median gap.
