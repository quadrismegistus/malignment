Metrical uncertainty and tension, human and LLM

Every point is a mean over 10-syllable windows cut at word boundaries, with lineation ignored, and scanned with the five constraints of the 2020 antimetricality study (s ≤ 2, w ≤ 2). Uncertainty is the number of viable scansions per window. Tension (MTS) is the sum of constraint violations across those scansions. Lower is more metrical. The first figure compares human and model prose and verse like for like. The second places every group measured so far against all the human baselines.

Alignment rewinds verse to the Augustans

Models were given the first five lines of a real poem and asked to continue it. Each line here is metrical uncertainty (viable scansions per 10-syllable window; lower is more metrical), plotted against the period of the poem being continued (the year its author turned 30). The thick grey line is human poetry of each period from the antimetricality data; its lowest point, 1700–1749, is marked. The poets' own continuations stay near their time; for 20th-century poems they land on their own period almost exactly. The models do not. Given a modernist poem (1900–49), the base models already continue it about as regularly as a Romantic poet (1800–49), and the aligned models as regularly as a poet of the late eighteenth century (1750–99). A post-war poem comes back from base models at a modernist level and from aligned models at a Romantic one. So alignment's own contribution is a further 50 to 100 years. Given an older poem, the aligned models (the two pairs pooled) bring it to about the regularity of Augustan verse whatever its period; this holds for Llama in every period but not for Mistral, which in 1800–49 sits above its base.

Canonical iambic pentameter, by model tier

Share of 10-syllable windows that scan as unambiguous iambic pentameter (uIP: the best parse is wswswswsws and it is the only viable scansion) and as perfect unambiguous iambic pentameter (puIP: that one scansion has no violations). Each row is a model continuing human poems after their first five lines, set against the poets' own continuations of the same poems. The tiers are different model families, not stages of one model: only the base → open-aligned step is paired within a model (Llama, Mistral). Below the continuations are poems written on request, by tier and prompt type (prompted to rhyme, prompted not to rhyme, neither). These have no poets' baseline and no base models, since a base model cannot follow "write a poem". "Without metre prompts" drops blank-verse, sonnet, couplet and pentameter prompts from the not-to-rhyme bucket.

Original, within-POS scramble, full scramble

Each text is measured as written (O), with its content words shuffled within their part of speech so the syntactic frame is kept (R), and with all content words shuffled (S). O minus R is how much more metrical the writer's own placement of words is than their frame alone; the R-to-S gap is what the frame contributes. Human texts are the antimetricality small data (one text per author, so no error bars). LLM prose is the national_story pure stories. LLM verse is continuations of human poems, pooled by tier, with the poets' own continuations of the same poems.

Hover a point or row for all values. Lower tension and uncertainty and higher uIP/puIP mean more metrical. In the lower panel, a filled marker is the original, a half-filled one the within-POS scramble, and a hollow one the full scramble.

AI verse sits at the Augustan minimum

Base and aligned models continuing the same human poems land at tension ≈ 4.7. That is as regular as the most metrical human period, 1700–1749 (4.44), and well below the poets' own continuations (6.46). The Olmo-3 pilot is an exception: it sits near 19th-century levels.

Base prose is more metrical than any human fiction

Base-model prose (9.42) sits below every period of human fiction (10.1–11.6) on both axes. Aligned prose (11.01) moves inside the human range, toward non-fiction.

Canonical pentameter climbs by tier

Continuing the same poems, base models write fewer unambiguous iambic pentameters than the poets (12.8% vs 16.3%). Open aligned models write slightly more (17.1%), and DeepSeek and Claude-3-Sonnet write 27–30%, near the 1700–1749 peak (28.8%) and approaching Pope (34.4%). GPT-3.5 matches the poets.

Alignment widens the verse/prose gap

Prose minus verse tension is 4.7 for base models and 6.3 for aligned ones. The human gap peaked at 6.4 in 1700–1749 and fell to 0.8 by 1950–1999.

Read with care

  • Verse and prose come from different prompts and generation setups. The verse continuations came from 4-bit ollama models; the prose came from full-precision HF generation with 3,000-token caps.
  • Only the prose results are a registered test (syntax_and_rhythm, H2). There, after controlling for monosyllables, aligned prose is not significantly more tense (13/34 lineages in the predicted direction), but it is more often imperfect on *s/unstressed (31/34, p = 7.7e-7). Everything verse-side is exploratory.
  • Periods run 1600–1999; 2000–2049 is left out everywhere, since it is not a full half-century and is sparse. The human prose curves are thin at the early end: Fiction 1600–1649 has 20 authors and 1700–1749 has 13.
  • The Olmo-3 pilot is 12 primers × 2 samples per rung. Its own docstring says it is never to be quoted as a result.
  • 0.14% of prose windows were skipped by the pronunciation-pooling guard. Windows parsed before the guard existed have not had the same rule applied retroactively.
