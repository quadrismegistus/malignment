# norms/ and fields/ — and where each file came from

**The split is functional, not by ownership.** RH: *"the relevant distinction is
lexicons/norms vs lexicons/fields."*

    norms/    a CONTINUOUS RATING per word — valence, arousal, dominance,
              concreteness, and our seven K scales. A number on a scale.
    frequency/  a CORPUS STATISTIC per word, added 2026-08-24. Not a rating:
              nobody judged it, and it is used as a CONTROL rather than as an
              outcome. Kept apart so "give me a rating" and "give me a
              frequency" stay different asks.
    fields/   CATEGORICAL MEMBERSHIP — this word is in this semantic field.
              General Inquirer, USAS, RID, WordNet supersenses, FrameNet.

That puts `k_ratings` in `norms/` where it belongs: seven continuous scales, not
field membership. Grouping by ownership instead would have split Warriner from
k_ratings while filing GI beside both, which is the wrong cut — a consumer wants
"give me a rating" or "give me a field", never "give me the ones we made".

**Correcting a commit message.** `5d78bb1` called this directory "ours". It is
not: roughly three quarters by size is third-party, and the README's own title
says so — *"External semantic lexicons for M01"*. Each entry below is
redistributable for research **with attribution**, which is why the attribution
is here rather than assumed.

## Why this file exists rather than a line in the README

A lexicon's terms travel with the file, and this repository is **public**. Saying
"ours" about a directory that is three-quarters Harvard, Princeton, Lancaster,
Martindale and ICSI is the kind of claim that is cheap to make and expensive to
have made. The per-file table is the only form that cannot be wrong in bulk.
